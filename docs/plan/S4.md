## 1. Frozen contracts (exact reference)

### 1.1 `notifications` (M8) — provider: API/DB · consumers: Infra, Web

Fields: `scheduled_at`, `due_version`, `dedupe_key`, `status`
(`pending|sending|sent|failed|delivery_unknown|cancelled`), `sent_at`,
`provider_message_id`, attempts, and a **redacted error** (no PII or payload). An expired
`sending` lease → **`delivery_unknown`** (uncertain delivery), **not** automatically
pending or system-retryable. `dedupe_key` = `(user_id, type, task_id,
due_version, occurrence)` for the scheduled occurrence — uniqueness prevents duplicates
**by construction**; a manual test send or warned resend adds `occurrence_seq` (1, 2, …)
inside the same identity (A4.2).

### 1.2 Reminder scheduling — provider: Infra

Timed (with hour): **T-48 h / T-24 h / T-2 h**. All-day: **T-3 d / T-1 d** at the user's
local time. No date → no reminders. Never a retrospective burst: changing the due date or
preferences cancels **only** the obsolete future occurrences. DST: nonexistent hour → next
valid instant; repeated hour → a single notification.

### 1.3 Preferences and M8 endpoints — API/DB area

`PATCH /me/notifications` (Gmail opt-in, digests, and thresholds) ·
`GET /me/notifications` (current preferences; read companion) ·
`POST /notifications/test`
(send to the verified email; `202` with a warning) ·
`GET /notifications?status=&from=&to=&limit=&cursor=` ·
`POST /notifications/{id}/resend` with `{acknowledge_duplicate_risk:true}`. Resending creates
a **new** audited occurrence and never mutates `delivery_unknown` to `pending`. Both a test
send and a resend carry an `occurrence_seq` inside the frozen identity (0 is the scheduled
occurrence), so the automatic dedupe stays exact.
`DELETE /me` returns `deletion_operation_id`; a post-tombstone failure stays `pending` and
resumable; `GET /me/deletion/{id}` exposes the closure without profile data and the scheduled
retention job deletes expired residual rows.

### 1.4 Digests — provider: Infra

Daily digest (07:00, configurable by local date) and weekly (Sunday) with the week's
conflicts (`time_conflicts` + `deadline_clashes` from the M5 contract). One digest = one
notification with its `dedupe_key` (includes the digest's local date).

## 2. API/DB tickets (`A4.x`)

### A4.1 — `notifications` + `settings` extension

**Depends on:** A3.8 · **Tests:** U-S4-AN-01/02.

`settings` already exists in S3 for Calendar; this migration only adds Gmail opt-in,
digests, and thresholds. `notifications` implements statuses, lease, persistent dedupe, and
redacted errors. `dedupe_key` uniqueness lives in the DB; do not create a second settings
table.

### A4.2 — Preferences, test, and resend endpoints

**Depends on:** A4.1 · **Tests:** I-S4-JF-05.

Implements `PATCH /me/notifications`, its `GET` read companion, `POST /notifications/test`,
the cursor-paginated listing, and `POST /notifications/{id}/resend` with mandatory
acknowledgement. The test uses the real/controlled-double pipeline; resend creates a new row
and leaves the original uncertainty intact. Migration `0016_s4_notification_resends` adds the
`occurrence_seq` component that makes a manual repetition insertable without weakening the
automatic dedupe (see §1.3).

### A4.3 — Cancellation of obsolete occurrences

**Depends on:** A4.1 · **Tests:** U-S4-AN-03.

How it must work: when the due date (new `due_version`) or preferences change, **only** the
obsolete future occurrences are cancelled (`status → cancelled`); already-sent ones stay as
they are; **never** a retrospective burst when confirming late.

Implementation status (2026-11-01): the pure core lives in
`apps/api/app/services/notifications.py`. `enabled_preference_types` maps the frozen
`[48, 24, 2]` hours / `[3, 1]` days thresholds and both digest switches to the schedulable
types (an offset without a frozen `reminder_t*` type enables nothing instead of inventing
one). `obsolete_pending` selects only `pending` rows: the reminders of a superseded
`due_version` scoped to their task, and the reminder/digest types a preference change
disabled. A task scope belongs to the due-date rule only: when both rules are passed, the
user-level digests are still evaluated against the preferences. A pending occurrence whose
instant already passed is selected too, so confirming
or editing late cannot fire the expired thresholds; `sending` (it may already be leaving
through Gmail) and every terminal state are never rewritten, and an explicit `test` send is
not preference-driven. `app/services/notification_cancellation.py` persists that decision in
the caller's transaction, re-checking `status = 'pending'` so a concurrent scheduler claim
wins and a second call is idempotent. U-S4-AN-03 is
`apps/api/tests/unit/test_notification_cancellation.py` (30 cases) plus the PostgreSQL proof
in `apps/api/tests/integration/test_s4_notification_cancellation.py` (7 cases), including a
combined due-date/preference call whose task scope never hides a disabled digest. A4.2's
preference endpoint and J4.3/J4.4 are the intended consumers of this seam; this ticket adds
no route, migration, provider call or `apps/web` change. A `digest_daily_time`/`digest_weekday`
change keeps the same type and dedupe identity, so J4.6 recomputes `scheduled_at` on the
pending digest instead of cancelling it: one digest per local date cannot be recreated under
the same key. Cancellation only ever selects `pending` rows; J4.3's adapter may re-arm a
cancelled scheduled occurrence when the same generation is scheduled again and the threshold
is enabled. That reversal is a scheduling operation in `reminders.py`, not a change to
A4.3's selection rule.

### A4.4 — Account deletion

**Depends on:** A3.8, J3.3 · **Tests:** I-S4-AN-04.

`DELETE /me` creates `account_deletions`, responds `202 {deletion_operation_id}`, applies
the tombstone, and blocks sessions/jobs. If event deletion was authorized, run/queue the
remote step before revoking tokens; then purge data and derivatives. A failure after the
durable tombstone still answers `202` and leaves a `pending` operation that the restricted
initiator can resume with `POST /me/deletion/{id}/resume` (Origin/CSRF checked). Execution is
serialized by an atomic claim (`pending|running` + lease); an already-succeeded remote delete
is never repeated. `GET /me/deletion/{id}` returns
`pending|running|completed|completed_with_remote_failures`; the residual record keeps no
profile, text, or tokens. A scheduled batched job deletes expired `account_deletions` and
`google_remote_operations` rows (30 days, D8 approved in A4.5). Double execution is
idempotent. `delete_maulwurf_events` defaults to `false`: Maulwurf-created Google events stay
unless the user opts in, and A4.5/UI must state that.

### A4.5 — Backup and restore validation (D8 already approved)

**Depends on:** A4.4, D8 approved · **Tests:** I-S4-AN-05.

The D8 policy is approved before non-synthetic data: backups contain encrypted text and
metadata only, retention is a 30-day maximum that matches the residual deletion window, and a
clean restore reapplies the deletion log so a deleted account, class, subject or conversation
does not reappear. The executable artifacts are `scripts/ops/backup_restore.sh` and
`docs/runbooks/restore.md`; the script fails closed if an unexpected binary column would put
audio or another payload into an archive. It never introduces audio or reopens the decision
by default.

### A4.6 — Final hardening

**Depends on:** A4.4, J4.2 · **Tests:** I-S4-AN-06.

Ownership verified in API/SSE/tools/jobs; `no-store` on private responses; basic rate limits
with `429/503` and correct headers (`Retry-After`) on sensitive endpoints (auth, upload,
chat, integrations). No invented numbers: limits per documented decisions.

Implementation status (2026-11-01): `app/core/cache_control.py` enforces
`Cache-Control: no-store` structurally on every HTTP response (routes, redirects, framework
`404/405`, rate-limit rejections and SSE); `app/core/errors.py` repeats it for the unhandled
`500` built outside the user middleware. `app/services/limits.py` adds a Redis fixed-window
limiter used by `/auth/google/start`, `/auth/google/callback` (hashed peer identity) and
`POST /integrations/google/{connect,reconnect}` (per user). Neither has a published numeric
decision (D4/D5 open), so both are configuration
(`MAULWURF_AUTH_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE`, `MAULWURF_INTEGRATION_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE`) and
unset means no limit, the same rule as the A2.3 upload quota. The documented limits stay
active and now always carry `Retry-After`: two chat streams (M7), the provisional search
quota (M6/D5), the upload quota/slot (A2.3/A2.14) and the 60 s Calendar cooldown (M5). A
Redis outage fails closed with `503 capacity_unavailable` and `Retry-After`. Ownership is
re-verified on API reads, the progress SSE and the index job (`revalidate`), and the full
matrix remains I-S4-AN-07; the chat tools are F2 and do not exist yet, so they are not
claimed. I-S4-AN-06 is `apps/api/tests/integration/test_hardening.py` (10 cases) plus
`tests/unit/test_limits.py` and `tests/unit/test_cache_control.py`. No provider call, no
migration, no audio and no `apps/web` change.

### J4.1 — Opt-in Gmail with `gmail.send`

How it must work: incremental OAuth adding `gmail.send` only if the user enables the option

### J4.2 — ARQ scheduler with a single role

**Depends on:** A4.1 · **Tests:** I-S4-JF-04.

How it must work: native ARQ cron scheduler with **a single active role** (elected via a DB
lease); every minute it computes due dates/local times and **claims rows atomically**;
multiple workers protected by uniqueness (`dedupe_key`) and leases; target delivery SLA
within 15 min (not second-level accuracy).

The election is the global `scheduler_leases` row (`name`, `holder`, `fencing_token`,
`lease_expires_at`, `heartbeat_at`). The holder renews its own row every tick; only an
expired row can be taken over, and a takeover increments the fence. Lease windows, expiry
checks and due comparisons use PostgreSQL's clock (`now()`) inside the same transaction, not
the wall clock of each scheduler host: two hosts with a clock skew cannot both consider the
same live lease expired. The table is granted to the worker/scheduler role only: neither the
API nor the ingest role can read or rewrite the election. The cron runs every minute and at
startup (so a restart drains what became due while it was down). The claim selects due
`pending` rows with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, moves them to `sending` under a bounded delivery
lease and never claims a row twice; it also persists the election's `fencing_token` in
`notifications.claimed_by_fence` (migration `0018`). The Gmail sender (J4.1) must compare that
value with the live election before delivering a claimed row, so a claim made by a superseded
scheduler cannot be sent under the current authority. An expired `sending` lease becomes
`delivery_unknown` (`lease_expired`) and is never returned to `pending`. `scheduled_at` is
already the absolute instant resolved from the user's local time by J4.3/J4.4/J4.6.

The scheduler is **disabled by default** with
`MAULWURF_NOTIFICATION_SCHEDULER_ENABLED=false` while J4.1 (Gmail delivery) is not deployed.
A disabled tick elects no role and moves no row, so a due occurrence is never claimed and
then labeled `delivery_unknown` for a send that never happened. Enable the flag only after
J4.1 consumes `sending`. Gmail delivery remains J4.1; this ticket stops at the claimed state
the delivery worker consumes.

### J4.3 — Reminder scheduling

**Depends on:** J4.2 · **Tests:** U-S4-JF-01.

How it must work: when a task is confirmed, schedule T-48/T-24/T-2 h (timed) or
T-3d/T-1d at local time (all-day) per §1.2; task without a date → no reminders;
occurrences are inserted with their exact `dedupe_key`.

Implementation status (2026-11-01): `apps/api/app/services/reminders.py` holds the pure
planner and the persistence adapter. `plan_task_reminders` only arms a `confirmed` task with
a coherent resolved date: `due_at` produces T-48/T-24/T-2 h from the instant, while
`due_date` produces T-3d/T-1d at the user's local wall clock; `missing`/`ambiguous` tasks and
unresolved states produce nothing. Thresholds already past at confirmation are omitted, so a
late confirmation cannot fire a retrospective burst; a threshold due exactly at confirmation
is kept because it is on time. `occurrence` is the user-local target day and `dedupe_key` is
built only by A4.1's `dedupe_key()`. A4.1 freezes no reminder wall-clock preference (only the
digest time), so the all-day default is the documented `09:00` local with an explicit
`local_time` override; the digest preference is deliberately not reused. DST normalization
remains J4.4. `schedule_task_reminders` builds the plan before writing anything, retires the
pending reminders of another `due_version` through A4.3's store, arms the new generation with
an upsert that targets `uq_notifications_dedupe_key` explicitly, and uses PostgreSQL's
`clock_timestamp()` (the statement instant, not the transaction start) when no instant is
injected. A repeated confirmation of the same version leaves a `pending` occurrence
untouched and re-arms a row A4.3 cancelled (a preference disabled and then enabled again);
`sending`, `sent`, `failed` and `delivery_unknown` never match the reopen condition and keep
their delivery history. The confirm endpoint (A3.4) is not part of `develop` yet; its
transaction is the intended caller and must bump `due_version` before scheduling. No
migration, provider call or `apps/web` change.

U-S4-JF-01 is `apps/api/tests/unit/test_reminders.py` (41 cases); the PostgreSQL adapter is
also proven by `apps/api/tests/integration/test_reminders_schedule.py` (12 cases:
exact rows/keys, idempotency, generation retirement, undated tasks, local all-day instants,
settings thresholds, preference-cancel/re-enable/re-confirm re-arming, no revival of
delivered or in-flight rows, the statement clock and the database-clock path).

### J4.4 — Persistent dedupe + DST

**Depends on:** J4.3 · **Tests:** U-S4-JF-02.

How it must work: `dedupe_key` uniqueness makes duplication impossible by construction;
changing the due date/preferences cancels obsolete occurrences (contract A4.3); DST:
nonexistent hour → next valid one; repeated hour → **a single** notification. Test cases
with injectable clocks crossing the time change.

Implementation status (2026-11-01): `apps/api/app/services/reminders.py` resolves every
all-day target day through `_resolve_local_wall`. The configured local wall clock is kept
across the change (only the UTC offset moves), a repeated wall clock (fall back) is armed
once at its first fold, and a nonexistent one (spring forward) moves to the first valid
instant after the gap — the transition instant — instead of the later "ghost" instant
Python returns by default. The resolution is deterministic and independent of the planning
clock, so re-planning after the change returns the same `scheduled_at` and `dedupe_key`.
`occurrence` remains the user-local target day and the canonical key carries no instant, so
a repeated hour cannot split one occurrence into two rows; A4.1's
`uq_notifications_dedupe_key` and the `UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT` tuple stay the final
barrier. A4.3's `obsolete_pending` still retires another `due_version` or a disabled
threshold, now proven on a DST target. J4.3's adapter already persists the plan in the
caller's transaction; J4.4 adds no migration, route, provider call or `apps/web` change.
U-S4-JF-02 is `tests/unit/test_reminder_dst.py` (28 cases) with injectable clocks around the
2026 New York transitions, the Lord Howe 30-minute gap and the Santiago midnight gap;
`tests/integration/test_reminder_dst_schedule.py` (4 cases) proves the persisted rows,
idempotent re-confirmation and generation retirement against PostgreSQL.

### J4.5 — Uncertain delivery handled

**Depends on:** J4.2 · **Tests:** I-S4-JF-05.

How it must work: lost response or expired lease → `delivery_unknown`, **no automatic
retry**. The panel explains the risk; resend requires acknowledgement and creates a new
notification with audited dedupe_key/occurrence without changing the original. Gmail does
not offer exactly-once.

Implementation status (2026-11-01): the delivery-side half is
`apps/api/app/services/notification_delivery.py`. `settle_delivery` locks one claimed
`sending` occurrence and settles it once: only a `NotificationDeliveryRejected` from the
sender is a known non-delivery (`failed`); an explicit `NotificationDeliveryAmbiguous`, a
lost response, a timeout or any unexpected sender exception ends terminal
`delivery_unknown` (`response_lost`). An expired lease is closed as `delivery_unknown`
(`lease_expired`) before the sender is called, and a retried settlement of a terminal row is
a no-op, so an at-least-once job cannot duplicate a send. The sender receives an immutable
`DeliveryRequest` (identity and schedule only, never the ORM row), and the settlement
enforces a caller-supplied `sender_timeout`, additionally capped by the delivery lease
remaining, so a slow adapter cannot outlive the claim; a timeout is ambiguous and ends
`delivery_unknown`. The pure classifier and error
taxonomy live in `app/services/notifications.py`; the Gmail adapter (J4.1) is still the
provider and no provider call is made here. The A4.2 resend endpoint remains the only way
back and keeps the original uncertainty intact; the web panel is D4.2.

### J4.6 — Digests

**Depends on:** J4.2 · **Tests:** I-S4-JF-06.

How it must work: daily at the configured hour by local date (07:00 default) and weekly on
Sunday; content: upcoming tasks + the week's conflicts; one send per user and period with a
`dedupe_key` that includes the local date; respects opt-in.

Implementation status (2026-11-01): `apps/api/app/services/digests.py` holds the pure planner,
the persistence/materializer adapter and the content builder. The daily occurrence is
`digest_daily_time` (A4.1's default `07:00`, minute precision only) on the user's local date,
and the weekly occurrence is `digest_weekday` (0 = Sunday, the frozen default) at the same
configured time because A4.1 freezes no second weekly time. Both local wall clocks go through
J4.4's `resolve_local_wall`, so a nonexistent hour moves to the first valid instant and a
repeated one is armed once. The planner arms the next occurrence strictly after `now`: today
if the wall clock is still ahead (or tomorrow/next week otherwise), so enabling a digest after
its hour does not send a retrospective one. `occurrence` is the daily local date, or the
Sunday that starts the local week for the weekly digest: a `digest_weekday` change inside the
same week recomputes `scheduled_at` on the same `dedupe_key`, and one weekly send per period
remains impossible by construction. The Gmail opt-in is a scheduling gate (no new occurrence
without it) and a delivery gate owned by J4.1 for an occurrence already armed, exactly as A4.3
records.

`materialize_due_digests` scans the opted-in `settings` rows as the cross-tenant jobs role,
cancels the pending digests the current plan leaves behind — an older period when its
`scheduled_at` is still ahead after a preference change to an already-passed hour/weekday,
or when the period is over, so a scheduler outage cannot flush a retrospective burst — and
recomputes a still-pending occurrence only when the plan actually moves its `scheduled_at`
(`attempts` is never reset, and a tick without changes rewrites nothing). A due
current-period row stays claimable by J4.2, a pre-armed later period is left for the plan to
adopt, a `cancelled` occurrence is reopened when the preference returns, and
`sending`/`sent`/`failed`/`delivery_unknown` history is never rewritten. The
`materialize_digests` ARQ cron runs every minute at second 20 and at startup, behind the same
`MAULWURF_NOTIFICATION_SCHEDULER_ENABLED=false` gate as J4.2, and stops at the `pending` row
the scheduler claims; Gmail delivery remains J4.1 and the observed send J4.10.
`build_digest_content` reads the tenant's active resolved tasks and the M5 conflict report:
daily content covers today and tomorrow plus their conflicts (M8), weekly content covers the
seven local days of the period and both `time_conflicts` and `deadline_clashes` for the week.
It returns task ids/titles/types/statuses and due representations only; no transcript text,
recipient or provider payload enters the digest seam.

I-S4-JF-06 is `apps/api/tests/unit/test_digests.py` (31 pure planner cases) and
`apps/api/tests/integration/test_digest_schedule.py` (19 PostgreSQL cases: local 07:00,
configurable hour/zone, Sunday default and weekday recomputation, idempotent materialization
with no rewrite on a second tick, the single J4.2 claim, opt-in, cancelled reopening, stale
and unscheduled cancellation after a past-hour/past-weekday change, a due row that stays
claimable, the preserved `attempts` counter, delivered history, jobs-role RLS and both
content windows with conflicts). No migration, provider call or `apps/web` change.

**Depends on:** S2.7 · **Tests:** L-S4-SG-01.

Definitive leases/heartbeat and fencing; 60 s sweeper; `audio_deleted_at` **only** after
checking the absence of temporaries (descriptors/processes/mounts, not just file names).

Implemented in `apps/ingest`: `supervisor.run_sweeper` runs on the service pool from the
ingest lifespan, one pass every `SWEEP_INTERVAL_S` (60 s; `INGEST_SWEEP_INTERVAL_S` ≥ 1 s),
sequentially and without overlap. `sweep_once` composes
`reconciler.reconcile_committed` with `reconciler.sweep_stranded`; every pass first re-reads
the durable admission latch (`admission.restore_from_database`), so a
`cleanup_status=failed` row persisted by another instance closes admission here within one
interval. The specification deploys a single ingestion instance for F0/F1
(`ESPECIFICACION.md`, F1 ingestion bullet), but the durable row is authoritative and the
per-pass read removes the restart requirement. A failed pass closes admission (fail closed)
and the loop keeps running, while an exception during recovery cleanup is recorded as
`failed` (durable) and halts admission instead of retrying silently. The durable `failed`
latch stays closed; a transient close caused by an unreadable latch is cleared by the next
successful read, and `halt` is idempotent per attempt so a repeated read does not duplicate
the alert. `SupervisorConfig` requires at least three heartbeats (`10 s` default) per `30 s`
lease, so one late heartbeat or a short event-loop stall does not fence a live attempt; an
unsafe interval is rejected when the config is built. `cleanup.absence_verified` gates
`audio_deleted_at` in `lease.record_cleanup` and `cancel.record_cancelled_cleanup` on the
descriptor/process/mount counters; a persisted `failed` cleanup is terminal, `lease.acquire`
refuses that row, and a later `failed` transition clears `cleanup_verified_at` and
`audio_deleted_at` so a failed row never claims deletion. The periodic loop does not depend
on a successful pool open at boot: `main.run_sweeper_when_pool_ready` retries
`open_service_pool` every interval until it gets a pool and only then starts `run_sweeper`,
so a database that was down during startup recovers without a process restart. When an
unverified cleanup cannot be persisted because the fencing token moved first (the stranded
sweep or a reclaim owns the row), or when the evidence write itself fails at the driver
level, the attempt stays `pending` and admission stays open; the supervisor or the periodic
reconciler raises the explicit `cleanup_unrecorded` alert and increments
`maulwurf_ingest_failures_total{stage="cleanup",error_code="cleanup_unrecorded"}` instead of
leaving only a log line (the operator procedure stays with S4.1, and the decision is noted
in `RUNBOOK-dev.md` §8). A driver error while recording recovery evidence is logged by type
and does not abort the pass, so `sweep_stranded` still runs for the remaining attempts. The
load leg of L-S4-SG-01 (concurrent uploads and injected failures) remains S4.3.
